# Dive: "Genius" differentiators for a deeply integrated task / reminder app

Date of research: 2026-11-01. Scope: innovative feature concepts, the evidence behind them, competitor coverage, solo-dev feasibility (Android + Ubuntu first, one cross-platform codebase), and server/LLM cost. Output: ranked top 10, a one-sentence product thesis, 3 signature features.

## How the grades work

- **verified**: I fetched or searched a source in this session. The URL is given. Where the search tool itself said it read only secondary coverage of a paper (not the paper), I write **verified (secondary)** and cite the secondary URL. The figure is real in the literature summaries but I did not read the primary text.
- **inferred**: reasoned from verified facts. The basis is stated.
- **unverified**: could not be checked. The reason is stated.
- **speculative**: product or design judgment with no checkable fact behind it.

## 1. The evidence base: what the behavioural science actually says

These findings are the "why" behind every feature below. A feature that cannot point to one of these is a commodity, not a differentiator.

| # | Finding | Grade + source | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Implementation intentions** ("When situation X arises, I will do Y") have a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment: d = 0.65 across 94 independent tests (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006). A later meta-analysis by Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer covers 642 tests (European Review of Social Psychology, 36(1), pp. 162–194; sources disagree on whether it is 2024 or 2025). Narrower domains show smaller effects, e.g. physical activity g = 0.31. | verified (secondary): https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/69905 , https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full , https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/brp/constructs/implementation_intentions/goal_intent_attain.pdf | A task should be able to carry a **cue** (the "when/where"), not only a due time. That makes the If-Then plan a first-class data type. |
| E2 | **The intention-behaviour gap.** A medium-to-large change in intention (d = 0.66) produces only a small-to-medium change in behaviour (d = 0.36) (Webb & Sheeran 2006, 47 experimental tests). People who intended to act but did not often cite **"forgetting"**. | verified (secondary): https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/107519/ , https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/c/6235/files/2019/02/prestwich-et-al-2015-implementation-intentions.pdf | Forgetting is the failure the app is paid to prevent. Reminder reliability is the product's core, not a feature. |
| E3 | **Prospective memory: event-based beats time-based.** Event-based tasks (triggered by an external cue) are performed better than time-based ones, which need self-initiated clock monitoring (Sellen et al. 1997). Reminders eliminated interruption effects on time-based tasks (McDaniel et al. 2004). | verified (secondary, mostly Wikipedia / encyclopedia summaries): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospective_memory , https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/31898 | Turn time-based intentions into **event-based cues** where possible: "when I get in the car", "when I arrive at work", "when I open my laptop". |
| E4 | **Reminders build repetition but not habit; event cues build automaticity.** In a 4-week study plus a review of 115 habit apps, relying on reminders supported repetition but *hindered* habit development, while event-based cues increased automaticity. Positive reinforcement was ineffective. Existing apps focus on self-tracking and reminders and "do not support event-based cues" (Stawarz, Cox & Blandford, CHI 2015, pp. 2653–2662). | verified (secondary: abstract text via search; UCL full-text page returned 403): https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/beyond-self-tracking-and-reminders-designing-smartphone-apps-that/ , https://core.ac.uk/download/79500911.pdf | Habit / routine features should **fade the reminder** and lean on context cues. Streak gamification is not supported by this evidence. |
| E5 | **Habit formation time.** Median ~66 days to 95% of the automaticity plateau, range 18–254, among the 39 of 96 participants whose curves fit well. One missed day did not materially change the curve (Lally et al. 2010). | verified (secondary): https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit , https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit | Use **forgiving streaks** (a missed day does not reset to zero) and set multi-week horizons for habits. Do not promise "21 days". |
| E6 | **Making a plan quiets unfinished goals** (Zeigarnik-style intrusion). Specific plans for unfulfilled goals removed the intrusive thoughts, high accessibility and task interference, without the goal being completed. The effect was mediated by how earnest the plan was (Masicampo & Baumeister 2011, JPSP 101(4), pp. 667–683). | verified (PubMed abstract): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/ | This is the evidence for **capture + "decide when"** and for an evening **shutdown review**: getting a task onto a concrete plan frees the mind. Blogs that stretch this to "shutdown rituals proven" go beyond the abstract. |
| E7 | **Planning fallacy.** Students' best estimate for finishing a thesis was 33.9 days; the actual mean was 55.5 days, and only ~30% finished by their predicted date. The bias was removed when subjects were told to connect relevant past experience to their predictions (Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994). | verified (secondary + course-archive PDF listing): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_fallacy , https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf | **Duration calibration**: log estimated vs actual time, then show "you usually take 1.6× your estimate for this kind of task". This is the "outside view" built into the product. |
| E8 | **Fresh start effect.** Aspirational behaviour (diet searches, gym visits, goal commitments) rises after temporal landmarks: a new week, month or year, a birthday, a holiday (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014, Management Science 60, pp. 2563–2582). | verified (secondary + author PDF listing): https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf , https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormnsc:v:60:y:2014:i:10:p:2563-2582 | Time the **weekly review, goal setting and "declare backlog bankruptcy"** prompts to landmarks (Monday, the 1st, the user's birthday). |
| E9 | **Habituation to repeated alerts.** People tune out repeated warnings (less neural activity, fewer eye fixations). Small visual changes raised adherence by ~20% (BYU security-warning research). A notification disrupts concentration for ~7 s, and notification count predicts disruption (2026 Computers in Human Behavior study, via secondary coverage). | verified (secondary): https://news.byu.edu/news/study-android-users-how-get-people-pay-attention-mobile-security-notifications , https://www.psypost.org/?p=231293 | **Nagging must escalate and vary**, never repeat the same alert. Notification volume has a cognitive cost, so the app must be **sparing**: few, high-value alerts. |
| E10 | **Interruptibility is predictable, best per person.** 10,372 in-the-wild notifications from 20 users: people click even highly disruptive notifications when the content is valuable (Mehrotra et al., CHI 2016). A model trained on a user's own data performs far better than a generic multi-user model, and reached >60% precision after nine days of training. | verified (secondary search summary): https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1502364/1/CHI16.pdf , https://typeset.io/pdf/designing-content-driven-intelligent-notification-mechanisms-35mu5sj3pp.pdf | **Snooze/timing intelligence should be personal and on-device.** A small per-user model or a simple statistical learner is enough; no cloud ML is needed. |
| E11 | **ADHD and time.** Time-perception problems are argued to be a core ADHD symptom. One account says the main deficit is time *reproduction/estimation*, not perception of intervals. Design guidance converges on **externalising time** (visual countdowns) and glanceable placement. The rigorous digital-tool evidence is thin; most of the material is vendor blogs. | verified (secondary, low quality): https://www.simplypsychology.org/adhd-time-blindness.html , https://evalab.artun.ee/dual-timer/ . A vendor-blog claim that 706 children improved with smartwatch reminders is **unverified**: no primary study was found, and the claim is dropped from the reasoning. | Visual time (shrinking countdown, "time until next commitment"), duration estimates on every task, minimal configuration to cut decision fatigue. |
| E12 | **Chronotype / time-of-day "energy".** A 2025 systematic review (Chauhan et al., Chronobiology International) found that >80% of studies showed **no main effect of chronotype** on cognition. A synchrony effect appeared in 29 of 64 studies of adults aged 18–45, mostly for attention, inhibition and memory, and in 10 of 12 studies of older adults. | verified (secondary): https://researchinnovation.kingston.ac.uk/en/publications/chronotype-and-synchrony-effects-in-human-cognitive-performance-a/ | "Energy-aware scheduling" rests on **weak/mixed evidence**. Offer it as a user preference ("deep work in the morning"), not as science-backed magic. |

## 2. Platform reality check (the constraints that decide feasibility)

Every "context trigger" and "nag" idea runs through these. They are the strongest feasibility facts in this dive.

| # | Constraint | Grade + source | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Exact alarms on Android 14+**: `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is no longer pre-granted to most newly installed apps targeting Android 13+ ("denied by default"). `USE_EXACT_ALARM` (granted on install) is for "calendar or alarm clock apps". `setAlarmClock()`, `setExact()` and `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle()` all need the permission or throw `SecurityException`. Check `canScheduleExactAlarms()` and send the user to `ACTION_REQUEST_SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`. | verified: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms | A reminder app **must** guide the user to grant exact alarms at onboarding, then re-check on each start. |
| P2 | **Play policy**: `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is a restricted permission. Qualifying core functionality is an alarm/timer app or a calendar app that shows event notifications. Apps that don't qualify "will be disallowed from publishing". | verified: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241 | For a public Play release, use `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` (user-granted). `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is a policy risk for a task app. Whether a task app with calendar display qualifies is **unverified** (it would need a Play review). The F-Droid / sideload build for the user can use either. |
| P3 | **Full-screen intents** (the "alarm screen" style nag): from 2025-01-22 Play enables `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT` by default only for calling and alarm apps. Others must have the user grant it (`canUseFullScreenIntent()`, `ACTION_MANAGE_APP_USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT`), plus a Play Console declaration. | verified: https://source.android.com/docs/core/permissions/fsi-limits , https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821 | An "unmissable" escalation tier is possible but is an **opt-in permission**, not a default. |
| P4 | **Geofencing**: limit of 100 active geofences per app per device user. Needs `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` plus `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION` ("Allow all the time") on Android 10+. Background response is "every couple of minutes". Recommended minimum radius is 100 m. A DWELL transition with loitering delay is supported. | verified (official doc via search): https://developer.android.com/training/location/geofencing | Location reminders are feasible but **coarse** (minutes, ~100 m). Fine for "at the supermarket", not for "at the door". Background location also triggers a Play declaration (**inferred** from the Play sensitive-permissions page listing Location: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16558241). |
| P5 | **Manifest broadcast receivers** (wake the app with no service running): `ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED`/`DISCONNECTED` and the Bluetooth headset/A2DP connection-state broadcasts are exempt, so **car/headphone connect can wake the app**. `BOOT_COMPLETED`, `TIMEZONE_CHANGED`, `TIME_SET` and `ACTION_EVENT_REMINDER` are exempt. `ACTION_POWER_CONNECTED` is **not** exempt, and **nothing Wi-Fi-related** is on the list. | verified: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/background-tasks/broadcasts/broadcast-exceptions | Bluetooth (car) trigger is **cheap and reliable**. Charger trigger needs a WorkManager `setRequiresCharging` constraint (timing loose). That workaround is **unverified**: I did not fetch the WorkManager doc. Wi-Fi trigger needs a different mechanism (a ConnectivityManager network callback; whether a PendingIntent callback survives app death is **unverified**). |
| P6 | **Activity Recognition Transition API**: `IN_VEHICLE` ENTER/EXIT callbacks via PendingIntent. Needs `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` on Android 10+. Google calls it lower-power and more accurate than polling. | verified: https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/location/transitions | "When I start/stop driving" is feasible **without** location permission. It depends on Google Play Services (absent on de-Googled phones; **inferred**). |
| P7 | **On-device Gemini Nano (ML Kit GenAI)**: the Prompt API ("direct model access") is **Beta**; speech recognition is Alpha; none is stable. Inference only when the app is the **top foreground app** (`BACKGROUND_USE_BLOCKED` even from a foreground service). Per-app and battery quotas apply (`BUSY`, `PER_APP_BATTERY_USE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`). Supported only on a device list (Pixel 9/10/11, many Samsung/OnePlus/Xiaomi models, …). | verified: https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai | On-device LLM parsing is a **nice bonus on supported phones, foreground only**. It cannot be the only parser, and it is not available on Ubuntu at all. |
| P8 | **On-device speech-to-text**: `SpeechRecognizer.createOnDeviceSpeechRecognizer()` and `isOnDeviceRecognitionAvailable()` were added in API 31. The default recognizer "may stream audio to remote servers". Language models may need downloading. | verified: https://developer.android.com/sdk/api_diff/31/changes/android.speech.SpeechRecognizer , https://developer.android.com/reference/android/speech/SpeechRecognizer.html | Private voice capture on Android 12+ is possible. Whether it works with no network is **unverified** (the docs only imply it). |
| P9 | **Android 16 Live Updates**: `Notification.ProgressStyle` gives "progress-centric" notifications with upgraded visibility and top ranking. Targets SDK 36. Official use cases are rideshare, delivery and navigation. | verified: https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/features/progress-centric-notifications | A **focus-timer or "next commitment" countdown** as a Live Update would be a strong time-blindness aid. Whether Play allows a *task timer* to be promoted as a Live Update is **unverified** (Google lists journey-style uses; there may be eligibility rules I did not fetch). |
| P10 | **GNOME global shortcuts**: GNOME had no implementation until **GNOME 48**, through xdg-desktop-portal-gnome and the `org.freedesktop.portal.GlobalShortcuts` portal. App-ID registration needs xdg-desktop-portal ≥ 1.19/1.20 (sources disagree). | verified (secondary, forum + packages): https://discourse.gnome.org/t/how-do-you-enable-disable-global-shortcuts-in-gnome-48/29119 , https://packages.debian.org/sid/amd64/xdg-desktop-portal-gnome | A global quick-add hotkey on Ubuntu Wayland is feasible on GNOME 48+. Which Ubuntu release ships which version is for the desktop dive. A fallback is a GNOME custom keyboard shortcut that runs `app --quick-add` (**inferred**: standard GNOME Settings feature). |
| P11 | **Google Calendar status events**: `eventType: 'focusTime'` with `focusTimeProperties` (auto-decline, chat status) is available on **primary calendars only and only to some users**. | verified: https://developers.google.com/calendar/api/guides/calendar-status | Auto time-blocking can write blocks as normal events everywhere, and as real Focus Time only where supported (Workspace accounts; **inferred** from "only to some users"). |

## 3. Candidate-by-candidate evaluation

Legend for **Server/LLM cost**: `none` = fully on-device/local; `sync` = needs only the sync backend the app will have anyway; `LLM` = per-call model cost (see §4 for numbers); `infra` = a dedicated extra service.

Feasibility = solo dev + Claude Code, inside one cross-platform codebase. Effort is in **developer-weeks**, inferred from typical scope. These are planning estimates, graded speculative.

## 7. Three signature features

### S1. Moments: if-then cues that fire at the right moment and won't let go
- Every task can carry a **Moment**: a time, a place (arrive/leave/dwell), a device (car/headphones Bluetooth connect or disconnect), driving start/stop, or a PC event (login/unlock/workday start). It is stored as a structured **implementation intention** ("When I get in the car → buy bread").
- Delivery is **escalating and varied**: a gentle notification, then a repeat at a learned cadence with a different sound and style, then an optional full-screen alarm tier. It runs until Done / Reschedule / Drop. Chronic snoozing triggers "break it down, move it, or drop it?".
- Why it wins: it is the only widely replicated, medium-to-large effect in the field (E1), it fixes the gap Stawarz et al. found in apps (E4), it respects habituation (E9) and personalisation (E10), and Android's Bluetooth/activity APIs make it cheap (P5, P6). No mainstream Android task app combines if-then cues with Due-style escalation (inferred from the competitor table; absence not exhaustively verified).
- Feasibility: ~6–8 dev-weeks for Android + Ubuntu; no server; no LLM (speculative).

### S2. Brain dump → Honest Day: triage, calibrated durations, a plan that re-flows
- Speak or paste a brain dump. The AI returns tasks with durations, dates and sub-steps as a **reviewable diff**. You accept, and the planner proposes today's plan against your calendar, using **your own estimate-vs-actual history** ("you usually take 1.5× on #admin").
- Morning: commit to a realistic day. Slip: one tap re-flows the rest (proposal-first, never moves meetings). Evening: a shutdown review closes loops and writes tomorrow's first step. Monday/1st-of-month: a fresh-start weekly review.
- Why it wins: it combines Tiimo's AI breakdown, Sunsama's ritual and Motion's scheduling, and adds the planning-fallacy correction (E7) that none of them shows. Evidence: E6, E7, E8.
- Feasibility: ~7–9 dev-weeks after two-way calendar sync exists. LLM cost is about $0.25/month per heavy user (§4).

### S3. Agent-native and local-first: your data, drivable by any AI
- A **local MCP server** ships with the Ubuntu app (stdio, over the local database). Claude Code, Claude Desktop or any MCP client can create, query, complete and plan tasks. A **remote MCP** (OAuth, spec 2026-07-28) comes with the sync service for mobile assistants. The app's own AI features call the **same tool surface**, one API for humans, the UI and agents.
- Data lives in on-device SQLite and works fully offline, with optional end-to-end-encrypted sync and self-hosting (Docker, which the user already runs). Cloud AI is explicit opt-in or BYO key.
- Why it wins: Todoist validated MCP demand, but it is cloud-only. The privacy-first, AI-drivable combination is rare among AI planners (Motion, Sunsama and Reclaim are SaaS-only; inferred). It is the natural fit for a solo developer working with an AI agent.
- Feasibility: local MCP ~1 week; remote ~2 weeks; the local-first sync foundation is shared with the core app (its cost belongs to the architecture dive).
